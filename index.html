WHERE THE OCEAN ENDS

20 Chapters Dual POV EN-US Playlist ✦

Adam Walker has the world at feet, an Oscar on his shelf, and a deafening void in his chest. After a devastating divorce that plastered his personal life across every tabloid cover, the Hollywood star flees into the shadows of Los Angeles in search of anonymity. He wanted to disappear. He just didn't expect to find her.

She is France's 'L'Ange Blond’. Madeleine Bennett, a prodigy heiress living under the constant, suffocating surveillance of a world that idolizes her but never protects her. With a history of abuse and control, Madeleine hides behind a shield of sharp sarcasm and her weed.

What begins as an undeniable physical attraction on her birthday night turns into a dangerous agreement: an escape with no tomorrows. But as the flashing lights of Hollywood pale before the raw honesty of their desire, Adam and Madeleine realize they can no longer pretend.

When the world demands they step back into the spotlight, they must decide if love is just an impossible script... or their only way out.

In Where the Ocean Ends, Tay Marais delivers a sweeping, deeply emotional romance about vulnerability, the cost of fame, and the courage required to trust again.

ADAM

Saturday, November 9, 2024, Los Angeles, CA.

The heavy wooden door slammed shut. A beam of light pierces the darkness, silencing Dreems do Fleetwood Mac’s music. An echo of the latch resonates within the deserted bar.

She doesn’t enter. She parades in. Stiletto heels strike the worn floorboards. As the dirty mirror reflects her bare legs, the image moves upward to the black satin dress that hugs her form.

My posture stiff’s. My shoulders shift, the stool groans beneath my weight, though my neck remains still. The scene is visible in the stained mirror above the bottles. An inch of my cap descends, masking the ice-blue of my irises but not my vision.

She practices each motion. Waves of champagne hair cascaded on her shoulders, a classical painting set against the gritty backdrop of Los Angeles. She’s trained to ignore the room’s gazes, not out of arrogance. Focus.

Once at the bar, she stops a few seats away. The atmospheric pressure shifts. A vanilla aroma, distinct from stale beer, makes the air heavy. My glance lowers. Invisible. Stay invisible. But she turns her face toward the counter light, invisibility becomes irrelevant. Recognition strikes with a harsh, unfeeling blow.

Madeleine. The ghost from Dylan’s stories, Thomas’s best friend. I always thought “Mad” was a stupid tabloid nickname until seeing her in person. What the hell is she doing in my sanctuary?

“Madi, what are you doing here?” Magnus’s voice breaks her silence.

My jaw clenches; shifting my gaze, I studied the amber liquid. I’m an actor. And she is performing her life. The bartender, who looks indifferent or hostile, smiles, and it’s an unfamiliar sight for me. As he moved around the counter, he wipes hands on the apron. Arms spread wide, embracing the inaccessible satin woman, as if she were his own flesh and blood.

“Magnus, it’s been so long…” Her answer is raspy against his shoulder. A slight crack which causes me to tilt my head. Interesting.

I shouldn’t pay attention. But observing human behavior is a vice, and I’m ‌absorbed in the scene unfolding by my side.

“Happy birthday!” Magnus pulls back to hold her hands with a familiarity that puts me on edge. “And I’m so sorry, my dear. Your grandfather was someone very dear to me.”

The contrast is violent. Madeleine pulls her hands aside to fiddle with her pinky. Her ring spins. I run my thumb over the pale band of skin where my wedding ring sat.

“Merci…” The tone drops to absolute zero. Cold, mechanical. Quick defense to not crumble at the mention of Henri Bennett. And it works.

“You’ve been too busy to stop by, haven't you?” The playful tone tries to soften the heavy atmosphere. He gets a quick laugh from her. “The usual?” As Magnus walks back, he reaches for the glass behind the counter.

“Yes, please. With lots of ice.”

She slides onto the high stool with a posture that is natural yet sculpted. She places her designer bag on the dirty counter without a care. Within sight, and yet control preserved.

Calculating every motion. The upturned nose is pure aristocratic disdain, the elegance screaming that no one should approach. But she's hiding a mine in the way she presses her lower lip between her teeth.

Madeleine Bennett.

The heiress. A French blonde angel that Hollywood devoured and spat out. She grew up in the spotlight and turned into a prodigy singer. We are both here, pretending we aren't what the world decided we are.

I force my eyes to the television in the corner and regain my focus, though my peripheral vision is still distracted.
