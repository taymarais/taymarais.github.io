Where the Ocean Ends

Adam Walker has the world at his feet, an Oscar on his shelf, and a deafening void in his chest. After a devastating divorce, he returns to the coast seeking silence.

ADAM

Saturday, November 9, 2024, Los Angeles, CA.

The heavy wooden door slams shut. A trail of light cuts through the gloom, drowning out the chords of "Dreams" by Fleetwood Mac. The sound of the latch echoes through the empty bar.

She doesn't enter. She parades in. Stiletto heels strike the worn floorboards with sharp, rhythmic slaps. Her bare legs guide the dirty mirror's reflection up to the hem of the black satin dress, which clings to her body like a second skin.

My posture locks. Without turning my neck, the stool creaks under my weight as my shoulders adjust. The stained mirror behind the bottles displays the scene. The brim of my cap lowers an inch just enough to obscure the ice-blue of my irises, but not my vision.

Every movement is rehearsed. Champagne hair falls in waves over her shoulders, a Renaissance painting in the grime of Los Angeles. Ignoring the few gazes in the room, not out of arrogance, but training. Focus on the objective.

She walks to the bar and stops two chairs away. The atmospheric pressure shifts. The air becomes heavy with vanilla that doesn't belong to stale beer. The brim of the cap lowers. Invisible. Stay invisible.

But when she turns her face toward the light of the counter, invisibility becomes irrelevant. Recognition comes like a cold, blunt punch to the chest.

Madeleine. The ghost from Dylan's stories, Thomas's best friend. I always thought "Mad" was a stupid tabloid nickname, until seeing her in person. What the hell is she doing in my sanctuary?

"Madi, what are you doing here?" Magnus's voice breaks her silence.

My jaw tightens. I shift my gaze to the glass, studying the amber liquid. I'm an actor. I recognize when someone is acting. And she is delivering the performance of her life.

The old bartender, whose default expression varies between indifference and hostility, breaks into a smile I've never seen before.

Coming around the counter, he wipes his hands on the apron. Arms wide open, embracing the expensive satin and the untouchable woman as his own flesh and blood.

"Magnus, it's been so long…" Her reply is raspy against his shoulder. A slight crack that makes me tilt my head. Interesting.

I shouldn't listen. But observing human behavior is a personal vice, and right now, I'm entirely hooked on the scene unfolding by my side.

"Happy birthday!" Magnus says, pulling back to hold her hands with a familiarity that makes me tense. "And I'm so sorry, my dear. Your grandfather was someone very dear to me."

The contrast is violent. Madeleine pulls her hands away to fiddle with her pinky. Her ring spins. I run my own thumb over the pale band of skin where my wedding ring used to sit.

"Merci…" The tone drops to absolute zero. Cold, mechanical. A quick defense so as not to crumble at the mention of Henri Bennett. And it works.

"You've been too busy to stop by, haven't you?" The playful tone tries to soften the heavy atmosphere. He gets a quick laugh out of her. "The usual?"

Magnus already starts walking back behind the counter, reaching for the glass.

"Yes, please. With lots of ice."

She slides onto the high stool with a posture that is natural yet sculpted. She places her designer bag on the dirty counter without a care. Close enough to be noticed, and far enough to maintain control.

Every motion is calculated. The upturned nose is pure aristocratic disdain, the elegance screaming that no one should approach. But she's hiding a mine in the way she bites her lower lip.

Madeleine Bennett.

The heiress. The "L'Ange Blond." The blonde angel that Hollywood devoured and spat out. A former child actress who grew up too fast under the spotlight and turned into a prodigy singer. We are both here, pretending we aren't what the world decided we are.

I force my eyes back to the old television in the corner, trying to regain focus, but my peripheral vision betrays me.

The tide is rising...
